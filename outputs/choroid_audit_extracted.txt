B. Exact Problem Statement and Motivation
1. Anatomy and Context
The Choroid: The vascular layer of the eye situated between the retina (specifically the retinal pigment epithelium, RPE) and the outer sclera. It provides oxygen and metabolic nourishment to the outer retina and avascular fovea.
Anatomical Boundaries:
Upper Boundary (Bruch's Membrane / RPE complex): A hyper-reflective continuous basement membrane separating the choriocapillaris from the retinal pigment epithelium. It exhibits high optical contrast on OCT B-scans.
Lower Boundary (Choroid-Sclera Interface, CSI): The transition zone where large choroidal vessels (Haller's layer) terminate and dense collagenous scleral tissue begins. It exhibits low contrast, optical speckle noise, and severe signal attenuation due to depth and overlying vascular shadowing.
Clinical Relevance: Variations in choroidal thickness correlate directly with major ocular and systemic pathologies:
Choroidal thinning: High myopia, geographic atrophy, age-related macular degeneration (AMD).
Choroidal thickening (pachychoroid spectrum): Central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR), polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV).
Why Manual Segmentation is Impractical: A typical macular volume contains 30–100 B-scans. Manual boundary delineation across a 3D volume requires 30–60 minutes per eye and suffers from high inter-grader variability (typical human grader disagreement at the CSI is 15–30 μm15\text{--}30\,\mu\text{m}15–30μm).
2. Conceptual Distinctions in Pipeline Outputs
Semantic Segmentation: Pixel-level binary classification generating a 2D probability map P^∈[0,1]H×W\hat{P} \in [0, 1]^{H \times W}P^∈[0,1]H×W indicating whether a pixel belongs to choroidal tissue.
Boundary Extraction: Delineating continuous 1D spatial curves zBM(c)z_{\mathrm{BM}}(c)zBM​(c) and zCSI(c)z_{\mathrm{CSI}}(c)zCSI​(c) for each A-scan column c∈[0,W−1]c \in [0, W-1]c∈[0,W−1].
Thickness Measurement: Column-by-column axial vertical distance t(c)=zCSI(c)−zBM(c)t(c) = z_{\mathrm{CSI}}(c) - z_{\mathrm{BM}}(c)t(c)=zCSI​(c)−zBM​(c), expressed in native pixels or scaled to micrometers.
Subfoveal Choroidal Thickness (SFCT): The localized choroidal thickness measured directly beneath the anatomical center of the fovea (c=cfoveac = c_{\mathrm{fovea}}c=cfovea​).
C. Dataset Facts and Verified Split Statistics
1. Dataset Origin and Structure
Dataset Name: OIMHS (Ophthalmic Image Macular Hole Segmentation Dataset).
Scanner & Modality: Heidelberg Spectralis Spectral-Domain OCT (SD-OCT).
Total Images: 3,859 composite scans across 125 eyes from 119 unique human participants.
Image Format & Dimensions: 8-bit PNG images of size 512×1024×3512 \times 1024 \times 3512×1024×3.
Composite Layout:
Left Half (512×512512 \times 512512×512): Raw grayscale OCT B-scan.
Right Half (512×512512 \times 512512×512): Color-coded manual multi-class ground truth.
2. Ground-Truth Color Mapping & Verification
Verified directly in src/dataset.py#L134-L146 and src/thickness.py#L150-L154:
Yellow (OpenCV BGR [0, 255, 255], RGB [255, 255, 0]): True Choroid (sits beneath Bruch's membrane, exhibits vascular voids).
Green (OpenCV BGR [0, 255, 0], RGB [0, 255, 0]): Neurosensory Retina (sits above Bruch's membrane).
Red (OpenCV BGR [0, 0, 255], RGB [255, 0, 0]): Macular Hole.
Blue (OpenCV BGR [255, 0, 0], RGB [0, 0, 255]): Intraretinal Cysts.

An anatomical audit (src/audit_thickness_distribution.py) revealed that Green had a mean subfoveal thickness of only ≈9.95 px\approx 9.95\text{ px}≈9.95 px (≈37 μm\approx 37\,\mu\text{m}≈37μm) and was positioned anteriorly, representing the detached/thinned retina at the macular hole. Technical Design Rationales
Why U-Net++ Over Standard U-Net: U-Net++ introduces nested, dense skip connections bridging the semantic gap between shallow encoder representations (edges, speckle) and deep decoder features. This enables feature fusion across multi-resolution paths before final boundary estimation.
Why scSE Attention:
Spatial Squeeze and Channel Excitation (sSE): Suppresses irrelevant spatial background regions while highlighting anatomical layer boundaries across A-scans.
Channel Squeeze and Spatial Excitation (cSE): Assigns global importance weights to specific feature maps that encode choroidal texture vs. overlying retinal strata.
Why Boundary-Weighted Loss: Because the choroid spans a broad central band, standard BCE loss is dominated by easy interior pixels. The distance-transform weight map w(x)w(x)w(x) exponentially scales loss within 5 px5\text{ px}5 px (σ=5\sigma=5σ=5) of Bruch's membrane and the CSI, driving sharp gradient updates at transition boundaries.

G. Post-Processing and Thickness Measurement
1. Selected Post-Processing Strategy
LCC-Only Retention: In src/postprocess.py, clean_mask() applies scipy.ndimage.label to extract connected components. The largest component (the continuous choroid bed) is retained, eliminating detached spurious blobs in the vitreous or deep sclera.
Disabling Binary Hole Filling & Savitzky-Golay Smoothing: An ablation study evaluated on the 697 test scans across all 18 patients (src/postprocess_ablation.py) demonstrated that binary hole filling and polynomial boundary smoothing significantly degraded thickness measurement accuracy:
Raw Predictions: Thickness MAE = 2.098 px (Baseline)
LCC-Only (Adopted): Thickness MAE = 2.095 px (p=0.3173p = 0.3173p=0.3173, non-significant difference from raw)
LCC + Hole Filling: Thickness MAE = 11.223 px (p<0.001∗p < 0.001^*p<0.001∗, statistically worse)
LCC + Hole Filling + SavGol (w=15w=15w=15): Thickness MAE = 11.307 px (p<0.001∗p < 0.001^*p<0.001∗, statistically worse)
Mechanism of Degradation: Hole filling artificially bridges anatomical vessel lumens (Haller's layer voids), pulling the estimated CSI boundary downward into the sclera. Savitzky-Golay smoothing flattens the natural subfoveal dip. Therefore, hole filling and smoothing are disabled in the final pipeline.
2. Continuity-Aware Merged-Band Algorithm
Verified in src/postprocess.py#L74-L191:
Lumen-Gap Merging: When a large unpainted choroidal blood vessel divides a column into two runs, if the vertical gap is ≤15 px\le 15\text{ px}≤15 px (max_lumen_gap=15), the runs are merged into an intact choroidal band.
Stray Stroke Rejection: Detached segmentation strokes separated by >15 px> 15\text{ px}>15 px (e.g. false positives across the macular hole) are discarded.
Rolling Midline Anchor: The expected choroid center is initialized as the median of all active rows (anchor_mid = np.median(active_rows)) and updated per column using an exponential moving average: prev_mid=0.8⋅prev_mid+0.2⋅(top[c]+bot[c]2)\text{prev\_mid} = 0.8 \cdot \text{prev\_mid} + 0.2 \cdot \left(\frac{\text{top}[c] + \text{bot}[c]}{2}\right)prev_mid=0.8⋅prev_mid+0.2⋅(2top[c]+bot[c]​) This ensures spatial continuity across shadowing columns.
3. Axial Scale and Foveal Gating
Pixel-to-Micrometer Calibration: Scale Factor=3.87 μm/px×496512≈3.753 μm/px≈3.75 μm/px\text{Scale Factor} = 3.87\,\mu\text{m/px} \times \frac{496}{512} \approx 3.753\,\mu\text{m/px} \approx 3.75\,\mu\text{m/px}Scale Factor=3.87μm/px×512496​≈3.753μm/px≈3.75μm/px This is derived theoretically from Heidelberg Spectralis nominal optical resolution. Because the public OIMHS dataset consists of 8-bit PNGs with stripped DICOM headers, this scaling factor is an unconfirmed assumption. All primary training losses, boundary MAEs, HD95, and thickness MAEs are computed in native image pixels.
Foveal Localization: In src/thickness.py#L150-L161, fovea detection relies on extracting the centroid of the reference manual macular hole annotation (Red BGR [0, 0, 255]). On an unseen, unannotated clinical OCT scan, the standalone CLI (src/predict.py) requires a user-supplied --fovea_col <int>. Standalone autonomous foveal pit detection without reference masks is not yet implemented.

2. Statistical Independence & Pseudoreplication
The Unit of Analysis: Primary metrics are reported as unweighted means ±\pm± standard deviations across all 697 test B-scans.
Pseudoreplication Risk: All 697 test B-scans originate from only 19 unique eyes (18 patients) (≈36.7\approx 36.7≈36.7 adjacent slices per volume). Because adjacent slices within an eye share morphological and optical features, treating 697 scans as completely independent observations represents statistical pseudoreplication.
Reporting Requirement: In academic presentations, results must be presented alongside the eye-level Subfoveal Choroidal Thickness (N=19N=19N=19 eyes), and scan-level standard deviations must be identified as slice-level sample dispersion rather than independent subject variance.

L. Inference and Demonstration Instructions
The standalone CLI script src/predict.py decouples inference from dataset split manifests and ground truth:
1. Verification of Capabilities
Accepted Input: Single-slice grayscale or RGB OCT images (arbitrary H×WH \times WH×W), as well as OIMHS composite side-by-side images.
Checkpoint Loading: Dynamically loads outputs/unetpp_r34_scse/best_model.pt (301 MB), reinstantiating architecture hyperparameters embedded in the checkpoint dictionary.
Resolution Preservation: Images are resized to 512×512512 \times 512512×512 for the forward pass, and the resulting probability map is bilinearly resized back to the exact input image dimensions before thresholding and boundary delineation.
Foveal Handling: If --fovea_col <int> is supplied, SFCT and parafoveal thickness are computed. If omitted, full-scan thickness is computed without guessing the foveal pit.
2. Live Terminal Command
To demonstrate inference live on an OCT scan:
bash
uv run python src/predict.py \
   --image "data/raw/OIMHS dataset/Images/39/10.png" \
   --checkpoint outputs/unetpp_r34_scse/best_model.pt \
   --fovea_col 256 \
   --output_dir outputs/predictions/live_demo
3. Generated Demo Files
The CLI prints metrics to stdout and generates 5 files in outputs/predictions/live_demo/:
10_mask.png: Binary segmentation mask (0 or 255).
10_overlay.png: 3-panel publication visualization.
10_boundaries_thickness.csv: Column-by-column coordinates of Bruch's membrane, CSI, and thickness in px and μm\mu\text{m}μm.

M. Limitations and Future Work
To ensure academic credibility, present these genuine limitations clearly:
Genuine Methodological Limitations
Four-Epoch Training Budget: The training budget was scoped to 4 epochs due to local Apple Silicon MPS constraints (~5.3 minutes/epoch). While sufficient for baseline separation, loss curves were still sloping downward; asymptotic convergence was not reached.
Clustered Observations (Pseudoreplication): The 697 test scans represent repeated cross-sectional slices from 19 eyes (18 patients). Adjacent B-scans within an eye have correlated anatomical features and signal attenuation. Scan-level standard deviations reflect slice dispersion, not independent patient variance.
Unconfirmed Physical Scale Assumption (3.75 μm/px3.75\,\mu\text{m/px}3.75μm/px): Because OIMHS images were distributed as PNGs with stripped DICOM headers, the physical scale factor is derived from nominal Heidelberg Spectralis optics (3.87×496/512≈3.75 μm/px3.87 \times 496/512 \approx 3.75\,\mu\text{m/px}3.87×496/512≈3.75μm/px). Primary evaluations are computed in native pixels.
Foveal Localization Gating: On unannotated scans, foveal position cannot be detected automatically by the network and must be supplied via --fovea_col.
Absence of External Multi-Center Validation: Models were trained and evaluated exclusively on Heidelberg Spectralis SD-OCT scans from a single hospital cohort. Performance on Zeiss Cirrus, Topcon DRI Triton, or Canon OCT-HS100 has not been evaluated.
Vascular Shadowing Vulnerability: Severe superficial vessel shadows extinguish backscatter at the CSI, causing network predictions to diverge from conservative human grader truncations.
Concrete Future Work
Multi-Task Joint Architecture: Incorporate a lightweight foveal-pit localization head to predict cfoveac_{\mathrm{fovea}}cfovea​ simultaneously from the internal limiting membrane contour.
Mixed-Effects Statistical Modeling: Implement hierarchical linear mixed-effects modeling or patient-clustered bootstrapping to produce cluster-robust 95% confidence intervals.
Cross-Vendor Domain Adaptation: Evaluate unsupervised domain adaptation (e.g. Fourier domain adaptation or cycle-consistent GANs) to translate Cirrus and Topcon B-scans to Spectralis feature space.
Bayesian Uncertainty Estimation: Implement Monte Carlo Dropout or deep ensembles to output an epistemic uncertainty map alerting ophthalmologists to unconfident CSI boundaries beneath vessel shadows.

O. Professor-Level Technical Questions and Answers
Data & Preprocessing
Q1: Why did you use CLAHE rather than standard global histogram equalization or unsharp masking? Answer: Global histogram equalization overamplifies speckle noise in the homogeneous vitreous cavity and washes out low-contrast choroidal boundaries. CLAHE operates on localized 8×88 \times 88×8 contextual tiles and caps contrast amplification at a clip limit of 2.0. This selectively boosts gradient magnitude along the faint, deep choroid-sclera interface without saturating the hyper-reflective RPE layer.
Q2: Why are vertical flips strictly forbidden in your data augmentation pipeline while horizontal flips are permitted? Answer: Retinal OCT B-scans possess a strict anatomical axial hierarchy: vitreous →\to→ neurosensory retina →\to→ RPE/Bruch's membrane →\to→ choroid →\to→ sclera. Applying a vertical flip inverts this physiological sequence, creating impossible anatomical priors. Horizontal flips, however, preserve axial layer order while simulating contralateral eye laterality (OD vs. OS), which is biologically valid.
Q3: How did you ensure zero data leakage between your training, validation, and testing partitions? Answer: We partitioned the 3,859 scans strictly by patient ID rather than image filename. Adjacent B-scans in a Spectralis volume are spaced only 30–120 μm30\text{--}120\,\mu\text{m}30–120μm apart, sharing vascular and morphological features. By grouping all scans from an individual patient (including bilateral eyes) into a single split, we verified via set intersection that Train∩Val=∅\text{Train} \cap \text{Val} = \emptysetTrain∩Val=∅, Train∩Test=∅\text{Train} \cap \text{Test} = \emptysetTrain∩Test=∅, and Val∩Test=∅\text{Val} \cap \text{Test} = \emptysetVal∩Test=∅.
Q4: Why does the test set have 19 eyes but only 18 patients? Answer: In the OIMHS demographics manifest (Demographics of the participants.xlsx), Patient ID 100 contributed bilateral eyes (both Eye 103 and Eye 104). Our splitting algorithm grouped at the patient level; therefore, both eyes of Patient 100 were assigned together to the test set, resulting in 19 eyes across 18 unique patients.
Architecture & Losses
Q5: What specific mathematical problem does boundary-weighted loss solve in choroidal segmentation? Answer: The choroid is a thick, continuous band occupying roughly 15–25% of the B-scan area. In standard cross-entropy, the gradient is overwhelmed by interior pixels that are easily classified, resulting in poor localization at the blurry CSI boundary. Our distance-transform boundary loss applies an exponential penalty w(x)=1+w0exp⁡(−d(x)2/2σ2)w(x) = 1 + w_0 \exp(-d(x)^2 / 2\sigma^2)w(x)=1+w0​exp(−d(x)2/2σ2) with w0=5.0w_0=5.0w0​=5.0 and σ=5.0 px\sigma=5.0\text{ px}σ=5.0 px. This concentrates gradient updates within a 5-pixel band of Bruch's membrane and the CSI, driving sharp boundary localization.
Q6: What is the parameter cost of U-Net++ with scSE attention compared to baseline U-Net, and where does that cost come from? Answer: Baseline U-Net ResNet34 has 24,430,097 parameters. Improved U-Net++ ResNet34 + scSE has 26,275,060 parameters—an increase of 1,844,963 parameters (+7.55%). This increase originates from two sources: (1) the dense nested convolution blocks at intermediate skip levels (X0,1,X1,1X_{0,1}, X_{1,1}X0,1​,X1,1​, etc.), and (2) the concurrent spatial and channel squeeze-and-excitation (scSE) modules placed at each decoder stage.
Q7: How does scSE attention differ from standard CBAM or Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE)? Answer: Standard SE recalibrates only channel dependencies via global average pooling. scSE concurrently combines Channel Squeeze & Spatial Excitation (cSE) with Spatial Squeeze & Channel Excitation (sSE). The sSE branch uses a 1×11 \times 11×1 convolution to generate a spatial projection map σ(Ws∗U)\sigma(W_s * U)σ(Ws​∗U), spatially gating background noise and focusing activation on the lateral choroidal layer regardless of channel depth.
Post-Processing & Geometry
Q8: Why did morphological hole filling cause catastrophic failure in your post-processing ablation study? Answer: In choroidal anatomy, large blood vessels (Haller's layer) create natural, unperfused optical voids where backscatter is low, appearing as "holes" within the binary mask. The human ground truth leaves some of these large lumens unpainted. When binary hole filling is applied, it fills these voids and merges stray scleral noise, expanding the lower border downward into the sclera. On our test cohort, hole filling increased thickness MAE from 2.09 px2.09\text{ px}2.09 px to 11.22 px11.22\text{ px}11.22 px (p<0.001∗p < 0.001^*p<0.001∗).
Q9: How does the merged-band algorithm handle vessel lumens without expanding into the sclera? Answer: Instead of 2D morphological dilation, the algorithm performs column-by-column 1D run analysis. If a column is split into multiple runs by a vascular void, it computes the vertical gap between consecutive runs. If the gap is ≤15 px\le 15\text{ px}≤15 px (max_lumen_gap=15), it bridges the vessel lumen. If the gap is >15 px> 15\text{ px}>15 px, it rejects the detached run as external noise by comparing the run midpoint to a rolling choroidal midline anchor.
Q10: Why did you disable Savitzky-Golay polynomial smoothing in your final evaluation? Answer: The fovea exhibits a natural physiological thinning ("foveal depression") where the choroid dips centrally. Polynomial smoothing across lateral windows (e.g., w=15–31 pxw=15\text{--}31\text{ px}w=15–31 px) flattens this anatomical depression, artificially increasing subfoveal error. Furthermore, raw boundary predictions from U-Net++ were already spatially smooth due to the inductive bias of convolutional decoders, rendering smoothing unnecessary and statistically detrimental (p<0.001∗p < 0.001^*p<0.001∗).
Evaluation & Statistics
Q11: Why is reporting Bland-Altman agreement on pooled columns statistically flawed in OCT analysis? Answer: Pooling all columns across all scans yields N≈350,000N \approx 350,000N≈350,000 points. Adjacent columns within an A-scan are highly autocorrelated, and all columns within a B-scan share patient-specific noise. Treating pooled columns as independent observations artificially inflates degrees of freedom, yielding falsely narrow confidence intervals. Bland-Altman analysis must be computed on per-scan means (N=697N=697N=697) so that each data point represents an independent summary measurement.
Q12: Is your test evaluation susceptible to pseudoreplication? Answer: Yes. Although we evaluated 697 B-scans, they were acquired from 19 eyes across 18 patients. Adjacent slices within a patient volume share anatomical features and signal attenuation. Consequently, the standard deviation of 0.02080.02080.0208 in Dice reflects slice-to-slice dispersion across volumes, not independent subject variance. In medical reporting, this must be acknowledged, and eye-level SFCT (N=19N=19N=19) should be highlighted alongside scan-level metrics.
Q13: Why is Bruch's membrane error four times smaller than the Choroid-Sclera Interface error? Answer: This reflects optical physics: Bruch's membrane sits directly beneath the highly reflective, pigmented RPE monolayer, generating a sharp, high-contrast step transition in optical backscatter. In contrast, the CSI is a diffuse transition from loose vascular connective tissue to dense collagenous scleral lamellae. Furthermore, light passing to the CSI is severely attenuated by multiple overlying retinal layers and blood vessels, obscuring optical boundary contrast.
Failure Modes & Limitations
Q14: Explain the primary failure case (Eye 39 Scan 8) and why the network diverged from the ground truth. Answer: Eye 39 Scan 8 was marked as is_shield = True due to a large superficial retinal blood vessel. The vessel absorbed and scattered incident OCT light, casting a dark vertical shadow that extinguished backscatter at the choroidal floor. The human grader conservatively truncated the choroid at 34.6 px34.6\text{ px}34.6 px depth. The U-Net++ model, utilizing contextual spatial features from flanking columns, interpolated an intact anatomical floor at 65.8 px65.8\text{ px}65.8 px depth, resulting in a CSI MAE of 33.30 px33.30\text{ px}33.30 px.
Q15: Why can't your standalone inference script (predict.py) automatically detect the fovea on raw unannotated OCT images? Answer: In the OIMHS dataset, ground-truth foveal scans were identified by detecting the red macular-hole annotation (extract_macular_hole_center). Our segmentation network was trained as a single-class segmenter targeting only the yellow choroid layer. Because the network does not segment the internal limiting membrane (ILM) or foveal pit, it cannot determine cfoveac_{\mathrm{fovea}}cfovea​ on an unannotated image without either an explicit user argument (--fovea_col) or an integrated retinal surface detection module.
Q16: How confident are you in the 3.75 μm/px3.75\,\mu\text{m/px}3.75μm/px conversion factor? Answer: We consider it an unconfirmed nominal assumption. The factor is mathematically derived from the Spectralis standard pitch (3.87 μm/px×496/512≈3.753 μm/px3.87\,\mu\text{m/px} \times 496/512 \approx 3.753\,\mu\text{m/px}3.87μm/px×496/512≈3.753μm/px). However, because OIMHS images were distributed as PNG files with stripped metadata, true voxel spacing cannot be verified from image headers. All primary model training, loss functions, boundary MAEs, and HD95 metrics were computed in native image pixels to ensure empirical validity regardless of physical scale assumptions.
